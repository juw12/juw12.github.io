The storyboard

One day, eight shots, eleven goals.

The goal card at the bottom of the screen works like the one in the game. Tap the marked spots in each frame to gather, plant, craft and trade. Recipes, yields, prices and goal rewards are the game's own numbers; only the waiting is sped up. Your progress is saved in this browser.

              Beyond day one

              The call sheet for the full game.

              Teduh (Indonesian for "sheltered, calm") is a cozy life sim on floating islands above the clouds. Here is everything the storyboard left out.

              Production: TeduhRuns in the browser, offline after first visit
              Locations
              Four islands. Home Isle and Market Isle are open from the start. Grove Isle opens when the sky bridge is mended, Reef Isle when the reef dock is built.
              Cast
              Sari runs the stall and sells seeds and hats. Bayu is the engineer who keeps the islands on sun and wind. Lin fishes and cooks. Villagers post orders every morning, and friends join you with a room code.
              Crops
              Basil, tomatoes, sunflowers and sky berries, each with its own growing time and yield.
              Water
              Fish your own pond, or the reef pool where the rare glass ray swims. Cook meals on a solar cookstove for helpful boosts.
              Weather
              A full day takes 10 minutes of play. Rainy days cut solar to 40 percent and push turbines to 150 percent, so a mixed island copes best.
              Goals
              19 guided goals, from chopping the first tree to lighting the old lighthouse so every island glows at night.
              Talking
              Co-op chat uses preset phrases, so there is nothing to moderate. Play in English or Bahasa Indonesia.
              Not included
              No account, no shop, no ads, no timers that punish you for being away.

              Under the hood

              How it's built.

              1. Plain JavaScript modulesNo build step and no runtime dependencies from the network.
              2. three.js r160, bundledThe whole world is built from primitives in code, which keeps it light enough for phones.
              3. Service worker and local savesAfter the first visit the game, its fonts and its pictures work offline. Your island is saved on your device.
              4. Opt-in co-op over WebRTCTrystero connects players through public Nostr relays. Playing together is opt-in.
              5. Tested in a real browserA Playwright suite with 86 tests, plus scripts that re-render the guide pictures and these storyboard frames from the game itself.

              The energy chart in shot 08 runs the same rule the game uses for daylight and power:

              // time of day t runs 0 to 1
daylight(t) = clamp(sin((t - 0.25) * 2 pi) * 1.5, 0, 1)
night       = daylight(t) < 0.08

// energy per second
rate = panels   * 0.06  * daylight(t)
     + turbines * 0.035
     - lamps    * 0.004 (only at night)

capacity = 20 + 40 * batteries

              Questions

              Before you go up.

              What do I need to play?

              A modern browser on a phone, tablet or computer. Open the link and play; there is nothing to install. You can add it to your home screen and it keeps working offline after the first visit.

              Is it really free?

              Yes. There is no account, no shop and no ads. The source is public on GitHub under the MIT licence.

              Where does my data go?

              Your island is saved in your own browser. Playing together is opt-in: when you share a room code, the game connects you to friends directly over WebRTC.

              Is the page progress the same as the game?

              No. This page keeps its own small save so you can try the first goals. The game starts you fresh on Home Isle with the same goals, recipes and rewards.

              Is it finished?

              It is live and still changing. The README always has the current state.

              Who made it?

              Juwen Lie, an R&D mechanical engineer in Batam, Indonesia, as an independent personal project.
